# Director's notes — The Entire History of Japan

**Thesis.** Japan survives by one habit: it opens the door, borrows everything, makes it its own, and sometimes slams the door shut.

**Promise (what the viewer clicked for).** The whole history of Japan in one sitting, and why a country can reinvent itself so often and so fast.

**Arc.** Hook (shock of rapid change) → wonder (islands, queens, tombs) → elegance (Heian) → violence (samurai, Sengoku) → stillness (Edo peace, closed door) → rupture (black ships) → overreach and catastrophe (empire, war) → resurrection (rebuild) → unease (decline) → resolve (the habit, the open question).

**World.** Paper and ink: hand-drawn plates on washi, brush characters, seals, sliding paper doors. The channel brand is "entire histories, told on paper".

**Central metaphor → visual throughline.** *The door.* A sliding shōji door (ShojiDoor) opens and shuts at every turn:
- Hook: opens on borrowing, slams on "slams the door shut".
- Sakoku: closes to a sliver; Dejima is seen through the gap.
- Meiji: thrown open on "copied from the best the West had to offer".
- Postwar: opens on "the miracle".
- Today: half open, the world (globe) waiting outside.
- Ending: open → shut → open on the series title.

**Other motifs.**
- *Dates in ink*: big hand-written years underlined in vermilion (1853, 794, 1603…).
- *Kanji seals*: culture montages get a vermilion seal per word (地震 火山 台風, 漢字 律令 仏教, 歌舞伎 浮世絵 俳句 相撲, 陸軍 鉄道 学校 憲法).
- *Kanji reveals* for the words the script asks us to remember: 神風, 戦国, 浮世, 明治, 鎖国, 廃刀令, 第九条.
- *The cast*: five polaroid cards in the hook (queen, court lady, warlord, shogun, train) return as the same cards in the ending.
- *Callbacks the script sets up*: "Remember that" (earthquakes) → 2011; "Remember those black ships" → chapter 8; swords by law (hook question) → 1876 seal; nine million empty houses (hook question) → final chapter counter.

**Entities to visualise (maps and graphics, no assets needed).** Tokyo Bay, Korean peninsula → Kyushu, Wei/Luoyang, Yamatai theories (Kyushu vs Nara basin), Daisen Kofun (Sakai), Chang'an, Nara → Kyoto, Kamakura, Mongol routes to Hakata, Sengoku daimyo map, Tanegashima, Korea 1592/1597, Tōkaidō, Dejima, Satsuma/Chōshū, empire expansion 1895–1937, Pearl Harbor, Pacific war, Tōhoku epicentre, Tokyo → Osaka Shinkansen.

**Tone rules for the war chapter.** No depiction of atrocities or victims. Nanjing and the atomic bombs get restrained ink cards (place, date, time) and the ruined Hiroshima dome. Somber muted grade, no flashy transitions, a drone bed, then silence.

**Script notes for the writer (not changed in the plan).**
- "Hold on to that word" (kamikaze) is never paid off in the narration. The plan pays it off visually (the word returns on the Pacific map at Okinawa, 1944–45). A one-line payoff in the war chapter would land it, e.g. *"In 1944, Japan gave that old word a new meaning: pilots who flew into enemy ships."*
- "Nearly fourteen thousand" islands: the 2023 official recount is 14,125, so "more than fourteen thousand" is accurate.
- Check before publishing: Daisen Kofun length (486 m), Edo population (~1 million c. 1720), Nikkei peak (38,915.87 on 29 Dec 1989), 2024 births (686,061, preliminary), 65+ share (29.3%), vacant homes (9.0 million, 2023 survey), 2011 dead and missing (~18,400).

**Voiceover direction (for OpenMontage TTS).** Leave a ~3 s pause after "slams the door shut." for the title card, and ~1 s after each chapter's first line is fine without extra pauses. Pauses requested by the plan are reported by `npm run retime` once the real voiceover exists.
