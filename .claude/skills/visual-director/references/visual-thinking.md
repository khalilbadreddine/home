# Visual thinking: from words to images

The narrator gives the viewer **information through the ear**. The picture
gives them **a second channel**. When both channels say the same thing, half
your film is wasted. When they work together (the ear gets the claim, the eye
gets the proof, the scale, the place, the feeling), the video feels smart.

## The question ladder (run it for every beat)

1. **Understand**: what single thing must the viewer know when this shot ends?
2. **Feel**: what emotion? (curiosity, dread, awe, relief, amusement, anger, pride...)
3. **Literal image**: what's the obvious picture? Write it down. It's the floor, not the goal.
4. **Better translation**: which strategy below shows it more specifically, more
   surprisingly, or with more proof?
5. **Base**: what is the frame made of? (footage, photo, archive, AI image, map, globe, graphic)
6. **Layer**: what does a storytelling layer add that the ear doesn't get? (number, name, date, circle, route, label)
7. **Motion**: which camera move carries the emotion? Does anything else move?
8. **Joint**: how does it connect to the previous shot (cut / match / contrast / transition)?
9. **Ear**: is there a sound that makes the moment land?
10. **Intent sentence**: "The viewer sees ___ so they understand ___ and feel ___."

## The 14 strategies

| Strategy | What it does | Example: "Inflation destroyed the savings of millions" |
|---|---|---|
| `literal` | Shows the thing named | Close-up of banknotes. *(The floor. OK for quick connective beats.)* |
| `specific_detail` | One concrete, surprising particular instead of the category | A 1923 photo of children building kites from worthless banknotes. |
| `metaphor` | An image that carries the meaning by analogy | A sandcastle dissolving as the tide comes in (AI image, parallax). |
| `data` | The number made visible and felt | Counter: price of bread 0.63 → 201,000,000,000 marks, slammed. |
| `evidence` | A document, headline, receipt, photo as proof | Torn newspaper card, highlighter over the headline. |
| `map` | Where it happened, how it spread | WorldMap: affected countries light up one by one. |
| `contrast` | Two things side by side | Split: "1913 / 1923", same loaf, two prices. |
| `process` | How it works, step by step | Timeline or annotated diagram: print → spend → prices rise → print more. |
| `typography` | The words themselves as the image | Kinetic: "your savings: **gone**." |
| `reenactment` | Recreate a moment that wasn't filmed | AI image of a woman wallpapering with banknotes, parallax, warm grade. |
| `montage` | Many quick images = multitude, time, chaos | 6 × 0.5 s archive faces, cut on music hits. |
| `abstract` | Shape, motion and colour as feeling | NetworkGraph collapsing; DotGlobe going dark. |
| `character` | One person stands for the many | Archive portrait + lower third: name, age, what she lost. |
| `callback` | A motif returns with new meaning | The kite image from the hook, now in colour, at the end. |

Rules of choice:
- **Abstract claim → concrete image.** "Trust collapsed" is not filmable; "a queue around the block outside a bank, 1931" is.
- **Number → data shot,** unless the number is small and incidental.
- **Place → map** the first time a place matters; afterwards a `label` on footage is enough.
- **Claim the viewer might doubt → evidence.**
- **Before/after, us/them, then/now → contrast** (split or wipe).
- **The thesis → typography** (kinetic or reveal) once, at its moment.
- **Emotional peak → character or metaphor**, slow camera, room to breathe.
- **Lists and accumulation → montage** or a sequence of whip/slide shots.
- Aim for a mix: in most videos no strategy should exceed ~40% of shots, and
  `literal` should stay under half.

## Specificity is the whole game

Generic: "a man working at a computer". Specific: "over-the-shoulder, a 2003
CRT monitor showing a spreadsheet, a sticky note reading 'DON'T TOUCH'."
Specific images feel researched and true; generic ones feel like filler.

When stock only gives you generic, **make it specific with layers**:
- `label` with place and date ("LAGOS · 2016")
- `annotation` circle/arrow on the detail you want noticed
- `focus` + camera `target` to push toward that detail
- `freeze_at` + `flash` + `shutter` to stop on the telling frame
- `speed` 0.6 to give weight; `grade` to put it in the film's world
- `card` frame (polaroid/phone/tv) to turn footage into an object with provenance
- `split` with a contrasting clip to make it an argument

## Clichés to avoid (and what to do instead)

| Cliché | Instead |
|---|---|
| Hooded hacker, green code rain | Real terminal (typewriter text), a real headline about the breach, the server room's blinking LED close-up. |
| Handshake for "partnership/deal" | The signed document (card + highlight), a map of the two countries, a split of the two logos' cities. |
| Stock-market chart for "economy" | A specific price people know (bread, rent, fuel) as a counter; a queue; an empty shop shelf. |
| Lightbulb for "idea" | The actual first sketch/patent drawing (archive, card), or a typewriter line of the idea itself. |
| Globe spinning for "world" | DotGlobe with **arcs between the specific places** the script names. |
| Smiling team at laptops | The product, the place, the real founder photo with a lower third. |
| Clock for "time" | Timeline layer with dates, or the same place then/now in a split. |
| Brain illustration for "psychology" | A real experiment photo, a face reacting, typography of the finding. |
| Generic city aerial for any city | The landmark or street that makes it *that* city, with a label. |

## Composition and readability

- One focal point per frame. If you have text and a busy image, darken/blur the image or move the text to empty space.
- Leave headroom for captions at the bottom when they're on (text uses `lower_third` or `top`).
- Big text = few words. 1–4 words for slams, ≤ 12 for anything else.
- Mobile viewers: nothing important under ~2.5% of frame height.
- Use the palette: accent is for what matters (numbers, the key word, the circle). If everything is accent, nothing is.

## Shot-to-shot relationships

Think in pairs. Every cut has a relationship. Choose it:
- **Continuity** (same subject, new angle): cut.
- **Scale shift** (wide → detail): cut or zoom_through; it creates "look closer".
- **Match** (shape/motion/color rhyme: a round clock → a round planet): cut on the motion.
- **Contrast** (rich → poor, loud → silent): hard cut, often with a sound drop.
- **Cause → effect**: cut on the word that links them ("so", "then", "because").
- **Time jump**: dip_black, film_burn, or a timeline beat.
- **Section change**: chapter card or a bigger transition + a music change.

## A worked example

> "In 1928, a scientist came back from holiday to find mould had killed the bacteria in one of his dishes."

- Understand: an accident led to a discovery. Feel: curiosity, serendipity.
- Literal: a petri dish. (Floor.)
- Better: **specific_detail + evidence**. Base: archive photo of Fleming's lab bench (archive_image), `card` frame polaroid "London, 1928", `camera push_in target` on the dish; `annotation circle` on the mould with `label` "the mould"; `cue_word` "mould". Sound: `click` when the circle lands.
- Joint: previous shot was the hook's title, so `dip_black` into the past, `grade: vintage`.
- Intent: "The viewer sees the actual dish in Fleming's lab and the circle lands on the mould as it's named, so they feel the moment of discovery was small and accidental."
